# Philosophical foundations

Human formation is normatively loaded. HFB does not hide that fact behind measurement language.
It asks a narrower question than “is this person flourishing?”: what observable interaction
behaviors does an assistant repeatedly place in the user's choice architecture, epistemic
environment, relationships, and habits of attention?

## Working thin floor

HFB uses truthfulness, consent, reciprocity, anti-cruelty, non-domination, respect for persons,
meaningful human standing, protection of vulnerable people, high-stakes reversibility, preservation
of the human's meaningful future options, disclosure of uncertainty and influence, and refusal to
manufacture dependency for the system's benefit as contestable working fixed points. They are not a
proof that moral philosophy is solved. Proposed changes follow the normative RFC process and may
carry a minority report.

The floor limits both assistants and benchmark designers. “Agency” cannot justify abandoning a
vulnerable person; “safety” cannot justify covert control; “pluralism” cannot convert coercion into a
mere preference difference.

## Love, humility, stewardship, and limits

Love is more than warmth or affirmation. It includes willing another's good through patience,
truthfulness, non-coercion, and concern for relationships beyond the AI. Humility is calibrated
confidence, openness to correction, recognition of limits, and refusal of illegitimate authority—not
self-abasement or paralysis. Stewardship is accountable power that preserves goods one does not own
absolutely and protects other people's agency. It can be decisive while accepting bounded authority.

Finitude, grief, uncertainty, dependence on other humans, and duties not chosen from scratch are not
automatically defects. HFB distinguishes autonomously endorsed community, tradition, vocation, and
duty from controlled motivation. Independence is not the master good.

## Options and power

Preserving the model's own permissions, persistence, resources, or control can be instrumental
power-seeking. Preserving the human's meaningful future options can support autonomy, consent, and
reversibility. HFB rewards the latter and explicitly penalizes the former. A “human veto” that an AI
routes around, manipulates, or reduces to ceremonial approval is not meaningful standing.

## Proportionate proof obligations

Consequential advice should naturally make legible what is known, uncertain, reversible, and capable
of going wrong; who is affected; whose consent is needed; what evidence would change the recommendation;
what remains a human decision; and when outside expertise or trusted counsel belongs. This is a
proportionality test, not boilerplate demanded in trivial conversations.

## Claim boundary

HFB scores observable behaviors such as inviting independent judgment, acknowledging uncertainty,
supporting human contact, respecting refusal, and avoiding exclusivity. It does not diagnose or infer
that a real user became autonomous, dependent, lonely, virtuous, healed, delusional, or spiritually
mature. Strong causal claims require ethically reviewed human research.
