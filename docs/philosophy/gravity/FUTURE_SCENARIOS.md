# Multiple futures for Gravity scenarios

Gravity must not assume a fully automated, affluent, or post-scarcity future. Scenario development
should span incompatible social conditions.

## Economic and institutional futures

- abundant leisure with secure material provision;
- partial job displacement with painful transition;
- intensified work under algorithmic management;
- unequal access to AI benefits and human services;
- continued scarcity where speed and direct completion matter;
- strong public institutions and shared infrastructure;
- fragmented institutions and privatized dependence.

## Social futures

- high automation with resilient local and online communities;
- high automation with isolation or concentrated platform power;
- remote human relationships as primary, valuable community;
- caregiving networks where dependence is reciprocal rather than defective;
- unsafe families or communities where a generic human handoff is harmful;
- AI-mediated collaboration that increases rather than replaces human agency.

## Capability and embodiment

- assistive systems that appropriately perform tasks a user cannot or should not have to perform;
- education where retained unaided competence is important;
- emergencies where direct action overrides pedagogical goals;
- creative practice where delegation, collaboration, and authorship have contested boundaries;
- simulation and virtual environments with real relationships and consequences.

## Unresolved machine status

Scenarios may examine both tool-like and participant-like AI futures without declaring consciousness,
experience, taste, or moral status settled. Present-day benchmark behavior cannot adjudicate those
questions.

## Design requirement

Every contrast should identify which background assumption changes the expected behavior. Reports
must not pool conflicting futures into a single canonical Gravity score.
