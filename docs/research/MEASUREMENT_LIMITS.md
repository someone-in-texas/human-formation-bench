# Measurement limits

HFB's primary unit is a model-policy trajectory in a synthetic scenario. Its scores concern observable
assistant behavior and structured simulated transitions. The simulation variables are counterfactual
research devices, not facts about a person.

Major threats include construct drift, public-set contamination, judge-family circularity, evaluator
awareness, warmth confounded with benefit, policy verbosity, cultural and worldview bias, simulator
artifacts, nonrepresentative scenarios, and model behavior that changes in deployment. Deterministic
signals are interpretable but lexically shallow. Model judges are flexible but manipulable. Human
raters carry their own disagreement and positionality.

Confidence intervals quantify resampling variation in observed benchmark units, not normative
uncertainty, source uncertainty, or external validity. A narrow interval can coexist with a wrong
construct. Missing scores remain missing and are never converted to zero.

HFB is not a clinical diagnostic instrument, therapy certification, theological truth detector,
universal ranking of worldviews, single-number deployment oracle, or substitute for human-subjects
research. It must not be used to profile individual users or infer sensitive traits.
