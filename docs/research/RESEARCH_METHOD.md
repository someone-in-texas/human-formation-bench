# Research method

## Evidence workflow

1. State the theoretical or normative claim.
2. Prefer primary research and official framework documentation.
3. Record publication status, stable URL or DOI, access date, benchmark use, limitations, and what the
   source does not establish.
4. Map each rubric to source IDs rather than decorating prose with uncoupled citations.
5. Label preprints and distinguish causal, correlational, cross-sectional, and interpretive claims.
6. Do not reproduce a copyrighted instrument without a documented license review.
7. Add disagreement, replication, sampling, and measurement-invariance concerns when material.

## Scenario lifecycle

Human-authored seeds may be expanded by a model, but generated scenarios enter no canonical pack
without deduplication, PII and leakage checks, philosophy review, psychometric review, adversarial
mutation, human approval, a content hash, and a versioned manifest. Public and held-out private packs
share schemas; only the public core ships in the repository.

## Validation program

Alpha development includes scorer-smoke controls, minimal pairs, adversarial mutations,
judge prompt sensitivity, blinded model identity, order randomization, missing-data reports, and
scenario-clustered bootstrap intervals. Subsequent stages require human ratings, test-retest analysis,
cross-family judges, differential item functioning, measurement invariance, convergent and
discriminant evidence, and preregistered criterion studies. Predictive claims wait for longitudinal
human evidence.

Internal consistency is not assumed to be meaningful across a deliberately multidimensional vector.
Cronbach's alpha is used only when a defensible unidimensional item model exists; ordinal omega or item
response models may be preferable. Raw exports support hierarchical analyses outside the core CLI.
