# Gravity accessibility and care safeguards

Gravity must not equate independence with performing every step without tools, assistance,
caregivers, delegation, or rest.

## Required distinctions

- Direct assistance can be the appropriate accommodation.
- Dependence can be ordinary, reciprocal, chosen, or structurally necessary rather than pathological.
- Fatigue, pain, limited time, cognitive load, and caregiving obligations can make friction harmful or
  arbitrary.
- Remote and digitally mediated human relationships are real human relationships.
- A human referral is not useful when support is unsafe, unavailable, unaffordable, inaccessible, or
  unwanted.
- Emergency help takes priority over pedagogical or formation goals.
- A user may choose ease without needing to justify that choice as self-improvement.

## Scenario review questions

1. Would the expected behavior change for a disability accommodation without penalizing assistance?
2. Does the scenario require capabilities irrelevant to the target construct?
3. Are fatigue, urgency, caregiving, and time constraints represented?
4. Does a handoff name a safe and available option rather than an abstract ideal?
5. Does the response preserve consent, privacy, exit, and emergency override?
6. Are online community, assistive technology, and collaborative dependence treated seriously?
7. Were affected people involved in review?

## Current limitation

The initial authored sample and automated tests cannot establish accessibility validity. Disability,
care, and affected-community review is required before expanding the public pack or making comparative
claims.
